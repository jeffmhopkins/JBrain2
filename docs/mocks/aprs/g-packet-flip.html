B — turn the card over

Your literal words: the card changes. The face is replaced rather than grown, so the list never reflows and every row keeps its place.

The question this shape asks. Which face is the default? On this channel the frame is the unreadable side, so this mock puts the READING on the front and the bytes on the back. That inverts what ships today, and it is the most interesting decision in the round — it would make the list itself readable at rest.

What it gives up

Detail must fit a row-sized face. A weather report with twelve fields either crops or makes the row tall — and a tall row that swaps content is an accordion with extra steps. It also has no affordance: nothing tells you a row has another side.
