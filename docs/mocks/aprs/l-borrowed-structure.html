aprs.fithe info page guide

The reference implementation, and the one every ham has seen. A station page gives the position in your preferred format, the Maidenhead locator, and a list of nearby towns with the distance and bearing from each — then last-heard time, course and speed, the most recent telemetry, the most recent weather, and the path with a digipeater advisor.

Taken Never a bare coordinate. aprs.fi always says where a station is relative to something a person knows — and it shows the grid and the distance, not one or the other. That is the “Range + grid” option above, and it is why the default here is not the raw latitude. Taken Its panel order — where, then when, then motion, then the station's own readings, then the path — is the order the expanded card now uses. Left Nearby town names need a gazetteer the box does not carry, and a network call it should not need. Distance from your box is the better anchor for a receiver log anyway: it tells you your own reception range, which is the question a heard log is actually for.

Its list row is three fields: timestamp and status on one line, then the message body below in monospace, at a larger size than the metadata above it.

Taken Monospace for what the station sent, proportional for what we say about it — the same trust boundary this card already draws with typeface. Good to see it arrived at independently. Left APRSdroid shows the packet body essentially raw. That is the thing your own screenshots made the case against, and the whole point of F5.

What none of them do

No client surfaces how the packet reached you on the row. They are mostly internet-fed, so “direct / gated / RF” is not a distinction they have to make. On a box with an antenna it is the difference between a station you can actually work and one that arrived over the internet — three quarters of your channel — so the badge stays.
