What a packet row shows

Shape D shipped, and your own channel broke it in three ways. Below: the faults, then three shapes for what a row should show and what it should hide — rendered on identical real traffic from the box. Rows edged in amber are reconstructed from your screenshots; the rest are captured packets.

1 · A position row said nothing

D's rule was “don't restate the icon as text”. But a plain position's whole reading is the symbol name, so the rule stripped it to empty — and the coordinates were a field, hidden behind a tap. The row lost the one fact it exists to carry. None of D's 58 sample packets hit this, because every one had a comment.

2 · Protocol bytes quoted as a person's words

The monospace slot means “the station's own words” — that is the whole trust boundary. /A=-00085 is an altitude extension and tU2k is a weather-software id. Neither is anything a human typed, and both were presented as if they were.

E · repair D

Same shape you have. Two things change: the reading is never empty — when the symbol name is all there is, the row falls through to the position — and the quoted slot only ever holds genuine free text.

Note it relaxes D's icon rule in exactly one case: when the symbol name is all the reading there is, the row prints it (“Phone at …”) rather than opening on a bare coordinate. The rule was never worth an empty row.

Keeps the scannable type column and the two-voice rule intact. Costs nothing new; it is the smallest change that makes your screen correct.

F · the reading leads

The reading becomes the title, in title weight; the packet type demotes to a small chip. The eye lands on what happened before what kind of thing it was.

Keeps every fact E shows, ordered by usefulness. Costs the type column — you can no longer scan a column of “Weather / Object / Position” down the left.

‹Radio

G · one line each

One line per packet: icon, reading, time. The station's own words, the provenance badge and every field move into the tap. Roughly three times as many packets per screen.

Keeps the whole window visible at once. Costs the quoted text and the direct/gated badge at rest — on a channel that is three-quarters relayed, provenance stops being glanceable.

What I need from you

Three decisions, and the third is independent of the first two.

  1. E, F or G — tap any row in each to see what that shape hides.
  2. Does the position belong on the row? All three now show coordinates for a bare position. The alternative is a place name, which needs a geocoder and a network call the box may not want — or nothing, leaving the row saying only “Position”.
  3. Collapse repeats? Switch to The 25 repeats above. Collapsing is honest only if the row says how many and how recent; it hides the fact that the object's content never changed, which is itself worth seeing once.
